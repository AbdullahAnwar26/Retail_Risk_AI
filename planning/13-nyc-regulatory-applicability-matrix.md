# 13 - NYC Regulatory Applicability Matrix (Federal + New York State + New York City)

**Status:** Planning / research artefact. **Retrieval date for all sources: 2026-11-09.** Law and guidance change often. Several items below were amended in 2025-2026 or are pending, and are flagged as such.
**Scope:** Retail Banking AI Intelligence subsystem (customer intelligence from transaction data, segmentation, churn, financial-needs identification, personalised product recommendations, retention insights, protected under-18 journey). It does not approve or reject credit or make regulated decisions. The LLM only orchestrates and words explanations (see `02-retail-subsystem-scope.md`, `08-security-privacy-compliance-requirements.md`).
**MVP jurisdictions:** US federal, New York State and New York City only. No other state rulebooks.
**Data:** Synthetic only. Most obligations below start when real customer data or real customer-facing outputs are introduced. Designing controls now avoids rework.

## 1. Purpose, disclaimer and method

### 1.1 Purpose
- Identify which federal, NY State and NYC laws, regulations and supervisory guidance **may** apply to the subsystem's activities.
- State the condition that decides each one.
- Turn each into **technical design requirements** the subsystem should support, so that whatever legal concludes, the controls exist.
- Produce a precise list of questions for the bank's legal and compliance teams.

### 1.2 Disclaimer
> **This is technical planning, NOT legal advice.** Every statement about what a law requires, whom it covers, or whether it applies is an interpretation that **requires confirmation by the bank's legal and compliance teams**. That holds even where it is marked [VERIFIED-SOURCE], which only means the source text was read, not that the reading is correct or complete. The bank's charter, regulatory status, licences, products, size and activities are **UNKNOWN**. Every applicability call below is therefore **conditional**. "Likely / Possible / Unlikely / Unknown" is a planning estimate to prioritise design work, not a legal conclusion.

### 1.3 Method and labels
| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[VERIFIED-SOURCE]** | The official or official-mirror text or page was opened and read during this session (2026-11-09). The URL is recorded. Only the specific points noted were checked. |
| **[PARTIAL]** | Some points in the row were read at source. Others (named in the row) were not. |
| **[UNVERIFIED]** | Comes from memory or secondary sources (law-firm or news summaries), or the source could not be opened. It is **not** to be relied on and is copied into the legal question list (section 6). |
| **[RECENT]** | Amended, issued or finalised in 2025-2026. Check for further change. |
| **[PENDING]** | Bill, proposal or litigation not final at retrieval date. |

Source notes:
- **eCFR (ecfr.gov) redirected to a bot-check page and could not be read.** CFR text was read on the Legal Information Institute mirror (law.cornell.edu). LII is a reliable mirror but **not the official publisher**. Legal should confirm against the eCFR / Federal Register official text.
- NY statutes were read on nysenate.gov (Open Legislation, which shows each section's "most recent revision" date). NYC Administrative Code (codelibrary.amlegal.com) **returned HTTP 403**. NYC points were read from nyc.gov (DCWP, CCHR) and the City Council's Legistar instead.
- Two PDFs (23 NYCRR 500 consolidated text and the SR 26-2 attachment) were downloaded from the official sites and read as extracted text.
- **Re-verification pass (2026-11-09, second pass):** rows F06, F11, F14, S01, S02, S08, S12, S13, S17 and C02 were re-checked against primary sources. Results and corrections are in the section **"Re-verification pass (2026-11-09)"** at the end. Corrected phrasings for other files are in **"Wording to propagate to other files"**. **Legal-point classification used in re-checked rows** (planning labels; legal to confirm):
| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **BINDING LAW** | Statute or regulation text (with legal force), if the institution is within its scope. |
| **SUPERVISORY GUIDANCE** | Agency guidance or industry letter. States expectations; by its own terms it does not create enforceable requirements. |
| **RECOMMENDED CONTROL** | A design or policy choice proposed by this planning work. Not required by the cited source. A bank policy decision. |
| **UNRESOLVED APPLICABILITY** | The source text is known, but whether or how it reaches this bank or this activity is open (charter, preemption, scope). |
